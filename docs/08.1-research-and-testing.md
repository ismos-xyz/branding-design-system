# 08.1 Research and testing

*Working. How ISMOS designs with the people it serves. Based on IDEO.org's Field Guide to Human-Centered Design.*

Every rule in this system is a guess until students and teachers have used it. This section sets how a change is researched, made and tested before it ships, so the system keeps learning.

## Three phases

| Phase | Question | What we do | What comes out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Inspiration** | Who are we designing for, and what do they need? | Interviews and observation with students and teachers in their own language, on their own phones. Immersion: take a course on a cheap phone on 3G. Talk to experts, including disabled students and teachers. | Quotes, photos (with consent), a short list of needs and the moments that matter. |
| **Ideation** | What could we make? | Read the research together, find themes, write *How might we…* questions, sketch several options (as with the four course card options), make rough prototypes, get feedback quickly. | Two or three tested options and the one we pick, with the reason. |
| **Implementation** | Does it work in real life? | A live pilot with one cohort or one page, measured; then iterate and widen. | A decision written into the right section, and an entry in 10 Accepted decisions. |

## Three lenses

Every change is checked against all three before it ships.

- **Desirable.** Do students and teachers want it? Ask them, don't assume. *Vibrant and happy* is tested with students in at least three languages.
- **Feasible.** Can it be built and kept up with the tokens, CSS and components here, on cheap phones, offline?
- **Viable.** Can ISMOS keep it going: a language owner, a template teachers can reuse, a cost we can carry?

## Mindsets

Empathy · optimism · creative confidence · make it · learn from failure · embrace ambiguity · iterate, iterate, iterate.

In practice: prototype before polishing, show work early, write down what failed and why, and treat every version (V1, V2) as a step, not an end.

## Minimum testing for a change

| Change | Who | How many | Methods |
|---|---|---|---|
| A new page, component or colour rule | Students | 5 per language, at least 3 languages, including Arabic | A 5-second test (what is this, is it for me), a task test on a 360 px phone on 3G, outdoors in daylight |
| Anything a student reads or presses | Disabled students | At least 2 per round | Screen reader (VoiceOver, TalkBack), text at 200%, Reduce Motion, keyboard only |
| Teacher material and templates | Teachers and ambassadors | 3 per round | Co-design session: they make a real slide or handout with the system |

People who take part are asked for consent first, can withdraw at any time, and are thanked for their time. Research notes never name a student without their permission.

## What we measure

- **Starting:** the share of new visitors who start a first lesson.
- **Finishing:** lessons finished, of lessons started, per course and per language.
- **Understanding:** a one-question check at the end of a lesson.
- **Feeling:** one question after a lesson: *Was this for you?* (yes · not sure · no), with room to say why.
- **Accessibility:** issues found per round, and how many were fixed before release.

Every figure is shown with its denominator and per language. No streaks, scores or leaderboards are used to measure students.

## Feedback and rhythm

- A *Tell us* link sits beside Help on every page (3.2.6), in the student's language.
- Teachers send what they see in their cohort through a short monthly form.
- Every six months the owner reviews research, feedback and figures, and decides what changes in the next version.
- Findings go into the section they change, and the decision is recorded in 10 Accepted decisions.
